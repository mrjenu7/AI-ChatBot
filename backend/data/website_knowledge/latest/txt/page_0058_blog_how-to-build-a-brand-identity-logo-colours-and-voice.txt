How to Build a Brand Identity: Logo, Colours and Voice
September 25, 2026
A brand identity is more than a logo. Learn the parts that make a brand recognisable: purpose, visuals, voice and the rules that keep them consistent.
A brand is the picture people carry in their heads about your business. A brand identity is the set of visible and audible signals that create that picture. A logo is part of it, but never the whole.
Start with the foundation
Before colours and shapes, answer a few questions in writing:
Who do we serve, and what do they care about?
What do we do better or differently?
Three words that describe how we want to be seen.
Who are we not?
The visual identity
Logo: simple, memorable and flexible. It should work small, in one colour and on a dark or light background.
Colour: a main colour, a few supporting ones and neutral tones. Colours carry emotion, so choose with your audience in mind.
Typography: readable fonts that match your personality.
Imagery and graphics: the style of photos, icons and illustration.
Layout: spacing and grids that make your materials feel related.
The verbal identity
Name and tagline.
Tone of voice: friendly, expert, playful or serious.
Key messages: the few lines you repeat everywhere.
Bring it together in a guide
A brand guide records the rules and examples: logo clear space, colour codes, font sizes and sample layouts. It keeps your team and your suppliers consistent.
Roll it out
Website and social profiles.
Stationery and documents.
Packaging and signage.
Presentations and sales materials.
Uniforms or vehicles, if you have them.
Pitfalls
Trend-chasing logos that date quickly, too many variations, and a brand that looks good in a presentation but cannot be used in daily work.

Frequently asked questions
What is the difference between a logo and a brand identity?
The logo is one element. A brand identity includes colours, typography, imagery, voice and rules for using them.
How long does a branding project take?
Often several weeks, depending on research, the number of design rounds and the number of materials.
Do I need a brand strategy first?
A clear idea of who you serve and what makes you different makes the design much stronger.
