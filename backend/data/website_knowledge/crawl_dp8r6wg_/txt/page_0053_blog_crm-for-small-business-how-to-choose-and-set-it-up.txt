CRM for Small Business: How to Choose and Set It Up
October 5, 2026
A CRM keeps every lead, customer and follow-up in one place. Learn how to choose one, set it up properly and get your team to actually use it.
If your leads live in e-mail, WhatsApp, a spreadsheet and somebody's memory, deals are being lost without anyone noticing. A CRM puts everything in one shared place.
What a CRM does
Stores contacts and companies with their full history.
Tracks deals through stages, from new enquiry to won or lost.
Reminds people about follow-ups and tasks.
Shows reports: where leads come from, how long deals take and what converts.
Before you choose: write your process
Describe how a lead becomes a customer in your business: where it arrives, who handles it, what the steps are and what information you need. The CRM should fit the process, not the other way round.
What to look for
Ease of use. If it is hard, people will go back to the spreadsheet.
Pipeline you can adapt to your own stages.
Integration with e-mail, website forms, calendar and accounting.
Mobile access for people who are out of the office.
Reports that answer your real questions.
Sensible price as your team grows.
Setting it up well
Start with a clean list of contacts; remove duplicates.
Create only the fields you will use.
Define the pipeline stages and what moves a deal to the next one.
Connect your website forms so every enquiry arrives automatically.
Set up a few templates for common e-mails.
Getting people to use it
Agree one rule: "If it is not in the CRM, it did not happen." Train the team in a short session, review the pipeline together weekly and remove any step that feels like paperwork.
Common mistakes
Buying a tool with too many features.
Not assigning an owner for the system.
Letting data become messy because nobody checks it.

Frequently asked questions
What is a CRM?
Customer relationship management software: a shared place to store contacts, conversations, deals and tasks.
When does a business need a CRM?
When leads or customers are tracked in several spreadsheets, inboxes or heads, and follow-ups start to slip.
How do I get my team to use it?
Keep it simple, make it the single place for sales information and show people how it saves them time.
