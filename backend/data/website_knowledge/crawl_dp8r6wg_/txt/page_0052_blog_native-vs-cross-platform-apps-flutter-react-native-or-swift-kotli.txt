Native vs Cross-Platform Apps: Flutter, React Native or Swift/Kotlin?
October 7, 2026
Native, Flutter or React Native? Compare cost, speed, performance and maintenance, and choose the right way to build your Android and iOS app.
One of the first decisions in an app project is how to build it. The choice affects cost, speed to launch and what the app can do. Here is a clear comparison.
Native apps
Native means writing separate apps: Swift or Objective-C for iOS, Kotlin or Java for Android. You get full access to every feature of the phone and the smoothest possible behaviour, at the price of two code bases and two sets of work.
Cross-platform apps
Cross-platform frameworks let one team build one app for both systems.
Flutter draws its own interface with a fast engine and gives very consistent design on every device.
React Native uses native interface parts controlled from JavaScript and suits teams that already know React.
What to compare
Cost: cross-platform usually needs less work overall.
Time to market: one code base means a faster first release and faster updates.
Performance: both are excellent for typical business apps; native leads for very heavy graphics or hardware use.
Look and feel: native follows each platform exactly; Flutter gives you one consistent custom design.
Maintenance: one code base is simpler to keep up to date.
Talent: consider who will maintain the app after launch.
Which one fits which app?
Business, booking, e-commerce and content apps: cross-platform is a strong choice.
Apps with advanced camera, AR, Bluetooth or heavy 3D: consider native, or a hybrid where only those parts are native.
An internal tool for staff: cross-platform or even a web app may be enough.
Do you need an app at all?
If your visitors mainly browse and buy occasionally, a fast mobile website or a progressive web app may serve them well. An app makes sense when people return often, you need notifications or you use phone features.
Plan beyond launch
Budget for updates when new phone versions arrive, for fixing bugs and for improving the app with user feedback.

Frequently asked questions
Is a cross-platform app slower than a native one?
For most business apps the difference is not noticeable. Apps with heavy graphics, advanced camera or sensors may benefit from native code.
Which is cheaper to build?
A cross-platform app usually costs less because one code base serves Android and iOS, but the final cost depends on features.
Can I start with one platform?
Yes. Many businesses launch on the platform most of their customers use and add the other later.
