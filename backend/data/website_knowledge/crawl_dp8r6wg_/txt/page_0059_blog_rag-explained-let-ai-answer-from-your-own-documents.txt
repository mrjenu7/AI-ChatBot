RAG Explained: Let AI Answer From Your Own Documents
September 24, 2026
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) lets an AI answer using your own documents instead of guessing. Learn how it works, where it fits and what it needs.
Ask a general AI a question about your company and it may sound confident and still be wrong. It was never given your price list, your contracts or your internal guides. RAG fixes this.
What is RAG?
RAG stands for retrieval-augmented generation. In plain words: before the AI answers, the system searches your own documents for the most relevant passages and hands them to the AI together with the question. The AI then writes the answer from that material.
Think of an assistant who looks things up in your handbook before replying, instead of answering from memory.
How it works
Your documents are split into small pieces and stored in a searchable index.
When someone asks a question, the system finds the pieces closest in meaning.
Those pieces and the question go to the language model.
The model writes an answer and can point to the sources it used.
Where RAG is useful
A support assistant that answers from your help articles and manuals.
An internal assistant for policies, HR questions and onboarding.
A sales assistant that knows your products, specifications and case studies.
Search across contracts, reports and proposals in natural language.
What you need to make it work
Clean content. Old, duplicated or conflicting documents produce confusing answers.
Clear access rules, so people only see what they are allowed to see.
A way to measure quality: a list of real questions with the answers you expect.
A plan to keep the index fresh when documents change.
RAG compared with fine-tuning
Fine-tuning changes the model itself and is useful for style or specialised tasks. RAG leaves the model alone and gives it fresh facts at the moment of the question. For business knowledge that changes, RAG is usually faster to build and easier to update.
Pitfalls
Poorly split documents, missing tables and scanned pages without text recognition are the usual causes of bad answers. Plan some time for preparing the data; it matters more than the choice of model.
Start with one knowledge base
Pick one body of knowledge your team repeats every day, build a pilot, test it with real questions and expand from there. We3vision can help you design and build it, from document preparation to a working assistant.
Frequently asked questions
What does RAG stand for?
Retrieval-augmented generation: the AI first retrieves the relevant parts of your documents, then writes an answer based on them.
Does RAG stop the AI from making things up?
It greatly reduces it, because the answer is grounded in your content, and good systems can show the source. It does not remove the need for testing.
Is my data used to train public models?
That depends on how it is built. A well-designed system keeps your documents in your own store and uses them only to answer your questions.
