Graphic Design for Business: Social Media, Print and Brand Assets
October 3, 2026
Consistent graphic design builds trust. See which design assets a business needs, from social posts to print, and how to keep them on brand.
Graphic design is more than making things look nice. It is how your business appears in every place where a customer meets it: a post, a leaflet, a presentation, a box. Consistency makes you look professional and memorable.
The core set every business needs
Logo in several versions: horizontal, stacked, icon, light and dark backgrounds.
Colour palette with exact codes for screen and print.
Typography: one or two fonts and rules for headings and text.
Image style: the kind of photos, illustration and icons that suit your brand.
Social media
Templates for posts, stories and covers so every post looks like yours.
A calendar of content types: tips, offers, behind the scenes and customer stories.
Correct sizes for each network to avoid blurry or cropped images.
Print
Business cards, letterheads and envelopes.
Brochures, catalogues and flyers.
Packaging, labels and signage.
Print needs high resolution, bleed and correct colour settings, so give your printer the files they ask for.
Digital documents
Presentation template with clear layouts for titles, text, charts and images.
Proposal and invoice templates that carry your branding.
E-mail signature and newsletter header.
Keep it consistent
Create a one-page brand guide with logo rules, colours, fonts and examples.
Store master files in one shared folder.
Use templates so non-designers can produce on-brand work.
Review old materials once a year and retire outdated ones.
Common mistakes
Too many fonts and colours, low-resolution logos, text that is too small to read and stretched images.
Design support
We3vision's design team creates logos, social media kits, print and digital materials. Tell us what you need and we will prepare a set that works together.
Frequently asked questions
Which design assets does a business need first?
A logo set, colour and font choices, social media templates, a business card and a presentation or brochure template.
Should I use templates?
Yes, for repeating content. A designer creates the templates and your team fills in the words and images.
How do I keep things consistent?
Write down a few brand rules and keep all master files in one shared place.
